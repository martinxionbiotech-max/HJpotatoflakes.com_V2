# Technical Data Governance

**Scope:** terminology, units, value classification and method conventions used across the HONGJI potato flake and powder technical documentation series.
**Applies to:** all documents in domains 01–09 of this knowledge center.
**Document family:** HJ-TD (technical documentation).

## Short Answer

Every number published on this site is one of six kinds of value: a **typical value**, a **guaranteed specification**, a **process target**, a **range**, a **batch-specific value** (COA), or a **customer-specific value**. They are not interchangeable. A guaranteed specification is the value a buyer can hold HONGJI to in a purchase contract; a typical value describes what production normally achieves; a COA value describes one delivered lot. Where a figure appears without a label, the label is stated in the table header or the surrounding text of that document.

## 1. Value classification (read this before quoting any number)

| Class | Meaning | How it is used in contracts and COAs | Example form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical value** | Normal production outcome, not a limit | Reference only — **never** a guaranteed limit | `6–8% moisture typical` |
| **Guaranteed specification** | Contractual limit declared in the product specification | Buyer-facing acceptance limit; verified per batch on the COA | `Moisture ≤ 9.0%` |
| **Process target** | In-process aim that keeps output inside the specification | Internal control parameter | `≤ 6.0% at the doctor blade` |
| **Range** | Observed spread for a defined grade or product | Grade description; individual lots may sit anywhere in the range | `Bulk density 350–500 g/L (loose)` |
| **Batch-specific value** | Analytical result for one production lot | Carried on the COA for that shipment only | `7.8 g/g — see COA` |
| **Customer-specific value** | Value agreed for a particular buyer's application | Set in the order confirmation / purchase specification | `Customised particle size to customer sieve curve` |

**Three rules that follow from the table:**

1. A typical value is never presented as a guaranteed specification.
2. A single COA result is never presented as a universal product specification.
3. No value is published merely to complete a table. Where HONGJI has no verified figure, the row is omitted or explicitly marked as not applicable.

## 2. Terminology and units

| Term | Definition used here | Unit convention |
|---|---|---|
| **Moisture** | Water content of the finished product, expressed on an as-received basis | `% (w/w)` |
| **Dry matter (DM)** | Solids fraction of raw potato, the inverse of raw-tuber moisture | `% (fresh weight)` |
| **Reducing sugars** | Sugars that participate in the Maillard reaction during frying or baking | `% (w/w)`, dry basis (`dwb`) where stated |
| **Total sugars** | Reducing sugars plus non-reducing sugars (chiefly sucrose) | `% (w/w)`, dwb |
| **Blue value** | Iodine-staining index used as an indicator of starch damage and free starch | dimensionless, absorbance-based |
| **Bulk density** | Mass of product per unit volume, measured loose or after tapping | `g/L`; `g/cm³` where the source document uses it |
| **Particle size** | Sieve or laser-diffraction characterisation of the powder or flake fraction | `mesh` **and** `µm`/`mm` (see §4) |
| **Rehydration ratio** | Mass of water absorbed per unit mass of dry product under a defined test | `g/g`, expressed as `x : 1` |
| **Water absorption** | Water taken up by the ingredient in a formulated system | `%` or `g/g`, method-dependent |
| **Colour** | Instrumental colour in CIELAB space | `L*`, `a*`, `b*` (D65/10°) |
| **Water activity (a<sub>w</sub>)** | Available water, distinct from moisture content | dimensionless |

Dashes and symbols: ranges are written with an en dash (`6–8`), limits with `≤`/`≥`, and ratios as `7.5 : 1`. Units are stated in metric SI form; `MT` means metric tonne (1,000 kg).

## 3. Specification conventions

- Specifications are declared in the **product specification** and restated in the **technical datasheets**; the specification governs where wording differs.
- Each specification row states the **test method** and, in the product specification, the **test frequency** (for example: annual verification plus per-batch COA).
- Test methods are named by their standard (for example `GB 5009.3`, `AOAC 925.10`, `ISO 4833`). Internal procedures carry HONGJI method codes such as `HJ-QA-025`.
- **Method comparability:** a value obtained by a Chinese national standard method (GB) and the same nominal parameter obtained by an AOAC or ISO method are not automatically interchangeable. Where a buyer's specification cites a different method, the method should be agreed explicitly rather than assumed to give the same number.
- Regulatory references: the potato flake specification is written against **SB/T 10752-2012 (Potato Flakes)** where a Chinese standard applies, plus customer requirements.

## 4. Mesh vs micron — do not interchange blindly

Mesh is a **sieve designation**, not a length. The micron equivalent of a mesh number depends on the sieve standard in force (ASTM E11, ISO 3310-1, Tyler and others define apertures differently) and only a defined aperture can be converted into millimetres.

HONGJI therefore states powder fineness in both terms together, from the same sieve table:

| Sieve mesh | Nominal aperture | Standard 60-mesh grade | Fine (80 mesh) | Ultra-fine (100 mesh) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 mesh | 425 µm | 100% passing | 100% | 100% |
| 60 mesh | 250 µm | ≥ 95% passing | 100% | 100% |
| 80 mesh | 180 µm | 50–80% | ≥ 95% | 100% |
| 100 mesh | 150 µm | 20–50% | 50–80% | ≥ 97% |
| 200 mesh | 75 µm | ≤ 15% | ≤ 20% | ≤ 30% |

Flake products are described by their **as-produced flake size** and by their **delivered sieve distribution**, which are different measurements: flakes leave the line at 3–5 mm, while handling and transport break a proportion of them into finer material, widening the delivered distribution to 0.25–3.35 mm. Both figures are correct; the distinction is stated wherever they appear.

## 5. Product grade naming

| Grade | Naming used | Particle size | Typical applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potato flakes | `Potato Flakes` | 3–5 mm as produced | Instant mash, bakery dough, extrusion, binder systems |
| Potato powder — standard | `Potato Powder, 60 Mesh, Standard Grade` | ≥ 95% through 250 µm | Dry mixes, seasoning blends, batter and breading |
| Potato powder — premium | `Potato Powder, 60 Mesh, Premium Grade` | ≥ 95% through 250 µm | Instant mash blends, clean-label formulations |
| Potato powder — fine | `Potato Powder, 80 Mesh, Fine Grade` | ≥ 95% through 180 µm | Sauces and gravies, lump-free thickening |
| Potato powder — ultra-fine | `Potato Powder, 100 Mesh, Ultra-Fine` | ≥ 97% through 150 µm | Beverage powders, cold-water dispersion |

Item codes (for example `HJ-PP-60-01`) identify a grade, its packaging and its minimum order quantity.

## 6. Variety naming

Varieties are named by their recognised cultivar name only: **Atlantic**, **Shepody**, **Russet Burbank**, **Innovator**. Company names — for example McCain Foods — are processors, not varieties, and are not used as variety names anywhere in this series.

## 7. COA interpretation

A certificate of analysis is a **lot-specific** document. It reports the analytical results for the batch actually shipped, tested against the product specification, and accompanies every shipment together with the documentation package (COA, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, bill of lading, packing list, insurance certificate).

When reading a COA, check in this order:

1. **Product and grade** — does it match the grade ordered?
2. **Lot / batch number** — does it tie to the shipment and to the traceability record?
3. **Method** — is the method stated, and does it match the method in the specification the order was placed against?
4. **Result vs limit** — a "pass" is a statement about the specification in force for that order, not a universal claim about the product.
5. **Conformance statement and release** — who approved the lot for release.

Our [Certificate of Analysis Guide](domain-05-quality-control/certificate-of-analysis-guide.md) explains each parameter line by line.

## 9. How to quote HONGJI figures

- Quote the **value with its class** — for example "guaranteed specification ≤ 9.0% moisture" is accurate; "moisture is 6%" is not.
- Keep the **test method** with the number when it matters to comparability.
- Keep the **document code and version** if you are quoting a controlled document.
- Do not convert mesh to micron without the sieve standard that produced the mesh figure.
